Our Culinary History

Tales of Indonesian Heritage

The Royal Cuisine

From Palaces to Everyday Tables

Indonesian cooking is a rich tradition spread across thousands of islands. From royal feast halls to everyday kitchens, its recipes focus on bringing out deep umami, natural sweetness, and comforting spices.

Nasi Goreng: Zero Waste Tradition
This famous fried rice comes from a thoughtful habit of never letting good food go to waste. Leftover rice was brought to life using sweet soy sauce and savory shrimp paste, turning humble leftovers into something extraordinary.
Wedang Jahe: The Javanese Remedy
In Java, Wedang means warm drink and Jahe means ginger. Originally served in the royal courts of Central Java, host families prepared this herbal drink to welcome honored guests with warmth, health, and vitality.

Prepared By:

Ablanque, Mendoza, Morales, Argame, Gaw, Mambaje, and Paglas
